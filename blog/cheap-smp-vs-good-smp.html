Cheap SMP vs good SMP: what the price difference buys you

I get messages every month from people who went the cheap route. The story is usually the same. The price looked great, the fresh photos looked fine, and six months later the dots spread, turned bluish, or the hairline sits way too low.

What you're actually paying for

  • The right pigment. Regular tattoo ink can shift color over time.
  • Depth control. Too deep and the dots blur. Too shallow and they fade fast.
  • Hairline design. A hairline that fits your face at 30 and still fits at 55.
  • Time. Good SMP is built over 3 to 4 sessions so it heals even.

What fixing it costs

Correcting bad SMP takes more time than doing it right once. Sometimes it means lightening or laser first. That's the part people don't budget for.

What to ask before you book

  1. How many SMP clients have you done?
  2. Can I see healed results, not just same-day photos?
  3. Who actually does the work?
  4. What happens if I need a touch-up?
